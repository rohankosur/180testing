004 / Courtside Math

problem of the week
Challenge #42 · AMC 12 / AIME Level 84 Students Solved · Current Week

Find the remainder when \( 2^{2026} + 3^{2026} \) is divided by \( 13 \).

A test of modular arithmetic intuition. Notice that 13 is prime, so Fermat’s Little Theorem guarantees that \( a^{12} \equiv 1 \pmod{13} \) for any integer \( a \) coprime to 13.

Step-by-Step First Principles Derivation:

1. By Fermat's Little Theorem: \( a^{12} \equiv 1 \pmod{13} \).

2. We divide the exponent \( 2026 \) by \( 12 \): \( 2026 = 12 \times 168 + 10 \). Hence, \( 2026 \equiv 10 \pmod{12} \).

3. This reduces our problem to: \( 2^{10} + 3^{10} \pmod{13} \).

4. Evaluate powers of 2: \( 2^4 = 16 \equiv 3 \pmod{13} \). Then \( 2^8 \equiv 9 \equiv -4 \pmod{13} \). Thus, \( 2^{10} = 2^8 \times 4 \equiv (-4) \times 4 = -16 \equiv 10 \pmod{13} \).

5. Evaluate powers of 3: \( 3^3 = 27 \equiv 1 \pmod{13} \). Then \( 3^{10} = (3^3)^3 \times 3 \equiv 1^3 \times 3 \equiv 3 \pmod{13} \).

6. Add the residues: \( 10 + 3 = 13 \equiv 0 \pmod{13} \).

Result: The remainder is precisely 0.
